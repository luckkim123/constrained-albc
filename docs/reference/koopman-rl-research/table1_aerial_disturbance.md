### [78] Folkestad, Pastor, Burdick — Episodic Koopman (KEEDMD), fast multirotor landing

**Mechanism.** Each episode *e*, the robot executes the *previous* episode's controller for 3 repetitions of takeoff→1s hover→land, plus one held-out evaluation landing, collecting a dataset with state, control, injected excitation noise, and reference. A neural network diffeomorphism `h(x)` is re-trained (warm-started from the prior episode) to fit Koopman eigenfunctions `φ^(e)(x)`; the lifted dataset from this episode is **appended to, not replacing**, all prior episodes' lifted data, and a single linear (elastic-net) regression re-fits the global lifted-linear model `ż = Az + B(u − û)` on the cumulative set. An MPC solved on this lifted model produces an *additive* correction: `u^e = u₀ + Σⱼ wⱼ uⱼ` with monotonically increasing per-episode confidence weights `wⱼ` — i.e., trust in newer episodes grows rather than the model being swapped outright. State/control bounds are enforced as hard constraints inside the MPC QP; there is **no Lyapunov or other formal stability certificate** — safety relies entirely on the nominal stabilizing controller plus constraint satisfaction.

**Disturbance representation.** Ground effect is **not** an explicit disturbance state or forcing term — it is absorbed entirely into the learned eigenfunctions/diffeomorphism as altitude-dependent nonlinear features of the lifted observable itself (`z=[p,v,φ(x)]`, with `φ̇=Λφ` once composed with the right diffeomorphism). There is no separate disturbance/uncertainty estimate analogous to our `z`; the *whole model* is batch-refit each episode rather than tracked online within an episode.

**Evidence.** Intel Aero RTF drone, OptiTrack+PX4 IMU fusion, all compute onboard (NN + MPC each ≈5 ms/eval). Only 3 learning episodes in the main result (5-episode cap per campaign), 5 independent campaigns for statistics. **19.3% tracking-error reduction** by episode 3 vs. episode 0, **+4.5% control effort**, and thrust constraints stay active longer in later episodes (evidence the ground-effect thrust boost was learned). Explicitly reported limitation: adding a controller term per episode causes **prohibitive computational growth**, which is why episodes were capped at 5 on this hardware.

**Maturity.** Real hardware, but narrow (single landing maneuver, near-1-DOF altitude channel), small campaign, proof-of-concept TRL.

### [86] Folkestad, Wei, Burdick — KoopNet, quadrotor low-altitude trajectory tracking

**Mechanism.** Learning is **entirely offline**: an encoder-decoder NN (2 FC layers, width 100, tanh) jointly learns the lifting `z=φ(x;θ)` (23 learned basis functions, with raw 12-D state also carried in `z`) and the bilinear model matrices `F, G₁…G_m, C^x` (plain linear layers), from 4 minutes of PID-collected data at 50 Hz (30% held out). Loss combines one-step prediction error in the *original* state space and one-step prediction error in the *lifted* space (multi-step consistency); the reconstruction term is explicitly **disabled** (`α=0`) since the raw state is already carried inside `z`, so no separate decoder is needed. **Once trained, the model is frozen** — deployed unchanged into NMPC. At runtime, only the frozen encoder is re-evaluated every 50 Hz control cycle on the newest state measurement to produce the current `z_t`; no parameters are updated online.

This is a load-bearing correction to the survey's phrasing: the survey text (p. 1099) says an NN is used "to update both the lifted states and inputs of the robot with online measurements." Per the paper itself, what happens online is ordinary per-step re-encoding (`z_t = φ(x_t)`) — true of any feedback loop — **not** online adaptation of the dictionary or bilinear operator. Flagging this because it directly bears on whether [86] is evidence for an online-adapting observer (it is not).

**Disturbance representation.** Ground effect and other unmodeled aerodynamic terms (`f_v(·)`, `τ_ω(·)` in the nominal model) are absorbed implicitly into the jointly-optimized lifted observables — again no explicit disturbance state, forcing term, or conditioning variable.

**Evidence.** Crazyflie 2.1 (33.6 g, thrust/weight 1.7), OptiTrack 120 Hz, offboard compute at 50 Hz. Figure-8 tracking at z=0.25 m: Koopman NMPC MSE 4.8e-3 m² vs. nominal NMPC 4.7e-3 m² (statistically tied at mid-altitude). At z=0.10–0.15 m the nominal NMPC begins destabilizing while Koopman stays stable; at z≤0.05 m **Koopman NMPC completes all 5 trials while nominal NMPC crashes catastrophically**. The Koopman controller also exploits ground-effect buoyancy to reduce commanded thrust near-ground versus nominal MPC.

**Maturity.** Real hardware, but this is offline system-ID for a *known, trained-for* disturbance regime deployed zero-shot — not evidence of generalization or online adaptation to disturbances outside the training distribution.

### [84] Shi, Teng, Kan, Karydis — Data-driven Hierarchical Control (DHC)

**Mechanism.** DHC wraps an **outer** controller around an *already-tuned, frozen* low-level controller + plant (treated jointly as a "new plant"). The model-identification block runs **DMDc** (Dynamic Mode Decomposition with control), but on the *reference-tracking* dynamics rather than the state dynamics: measured state `x_t` is treated as DMDc's "input," and the refined-reference sequence `r̂_t` as its "evolving output," fitting `r̂_{t+1} = Â_t r̂_t + B̂_t x_t`. The outer controller then reshapes the reference actually sent to the frozen inner controller: `r̂_{t+1} = Â_t r̂_t + B̂_t r_{t+1}`. This is **genuinely online, recursive, per-timestep re-identification**: initialize over the first `M = N_x+N_u+1` steps (the minimum for a well-posed regression), then at every subsequent step check the rank of the measurement matrix — if full column rank, re-run the DMDc regression; **if not, freeze `(Â,B̂)` at the previous estimate** (an explicit ill-conditioning safeguard). This is the only one of the three papers with a **formal stability guarantee**: a discrete Lyapunov argument (`V=xᵀx`) proves that wrapping DHC around an already-stable inner loop cannot destabilize it, because the outer correction collapses to zero at the reference/origin.

**Disturbance representation.** Uncertainty `w_t` in the plant propagation `x_{t+1}=g(r̂_{t+1},w_t)` is **never modeled explicitly** — instead its *effect on achievable reference tracking* is captured directly as a continuously-refit linear corrective operator in reference-space, not lifted-state space and not a separate latent estimate.

**Evidence — simulation.** Planar quadrotor linearized about hover, parameter-matched to a real Crazyflie (m=0.03 kg, I_xx=1.43e-5 kg·m²), mass/inertia perturbed once per trial (low noise: σ_m=0.2m, σ_Ixx=0.2·I_xx; high noise: σ_m=0.6m, σ_Ixx=0.6·I_xx — i.e., episodic randomized-dynamics DR, structurally similar to our per-episode plant randomization), 10 trials/condition, compared against state-feedback (SFC), linear MPC, and MRAC. At high noise: SFC goes **unstable**; MPC settles fast (0.04s) but with large steady-state error [1.70, 1.19]; MRAC gets steady-state error down to [1.04,1.04] but needs 11.08 control effort and 0.92s settling with overshoot; **DHC achieves steady-state error [0, 0.0085] (near-zero) with only 3.61 control effort and 0.38s settling** — better accuracy than MRAC at roughly a third of the control effort. The headline result, stated explicitly by the authors: DHC's steady-state error and control effort barely change between low- and high-noise conditions, because "the disturbance gets embedded in the learned model."

**Evidence — hardware.** Crazyflie 2.1, VICON mocap, tested wrapping DHC around *two different* pre-tuned low-level controllers (nonlinear geometric, PID). Ground-effect task: 2.4 m constant-height flight over an obstacle (h_o=0.29 m), at 3 clearances × 6 forward speeds (0.4–1.4 m/s) × 4 controller configs × 10 trials = **720 real flight trials**. DHC-wrapped controllers stay closer to the desired height on average, with the largest gains at low clearance and high forward speed (where ground-effect/drag coupling is strongest and least modeled). Explicitly reported limitation: DHC remains bound by the *inner* controller's authority — after clearing the obstacle, post-obstacle steady-state error still increases at higher speeds even with DHC active, i.e., the outer loop cannot fully compensate for inner-loop bandwidth/tuning limits.

**Maturity.** Real hardware, the largest trial count of the three (720), and the only formal (if narrow) theoretical guarantee. This is the most directly "plug-in deployable" of the trio — consistent with the survey's own Table I classification of it as "Adaptive Controller."

## Implications for our three design items

**(1) Deployment-time online Koopman disturbance/current observer feeding the student.** [84]/DHC is the only mechanism among the three actually shaped like a runtime observer: continuous per-step re-identification with a rank-conditioned freeze safeguard and a Lyapunov argument that the wrapper cannot destabilize the underlying (student) policy. Directly transferable ideas: (a) the freeze-on-rank-deficiency safeguard as a cheap numerical guard for an online regression-based current/disturbance estimator; (b) the proof pattern that the correction term must collapse to zero at the reference/nominal point — our observer's contribution should analogously vanish when current is zero, to inherit a similar non-destabilization argument. Caveats that must be validated, not assumed: DHC was demonstrated on a low-dimensional (6-state, scalar-ish height-hold) plant under continuous, slowly-varying parametric noise sampled once per trial — not our full 6-DOF attitude problem with rapidly time-varying current *and* discrete episodic thruster faults (abrupt jumps, not drift). Neither scale nor fault-type generalization is evidenced here. [78]/KEEDMD is *episodic* (between-trial batch refit), not within-episode online estimation — wrong mechanical shape for a deployment-time observer, though its confidence-weighted additive blending (`u^e=u₀+Σwⱼuⱼ`) is a reusable pattern for smoothly blending an online-adapted correction with a frozen nominal policy. [86]/KoopNet is offline-trained and frozen at deployment — the survey's "updates with online measurements" phrasing refers only to ordinary per-step re-encoding of a fixed function, not parameter adaptation; it is evidence that a well-trained fixed lifting generalizes zero-shot *within its training regime*, not evidence for an online-adapting observer.

**(2) KIPPO-style learned lifting φ_x with aux recon+prediction, block-partitioned targets.** None of the three papers factor disturbance out into an explicit conditioning variable analogous to our `z` — all three fold it implicitly into the lifted observables/operator instead. That is weak positive evidence that a sufficiently expressive lifting *can* absorb disturbance-relevant structure without explicit factoring (KEEDMD's per-episode eigenfunction refit, KoopNet's joint dictionary learning both demonstrably captured ground effect), but it is **not** evidence either way on whether explicit factoring (our approach) outperforms implicit absorption — no paper here tests that comparison. One concretely transferable detail: [86]'s loss recipe (disable reconstruction when raw state is already appended to the lifted vector; keep a dual state-space + lifted-space one-step prediction consistency loss) is a minor, directly reusable design point for our aux-prediction loss if φ_x similarly augments rather than replaces raw obs. [84]'s state-as-input/reference-as-output DMDc split is a loose, shallow 2-block analogy to "partitioned prediction targets" — not a real precedent for rich block-structured latent prediction; do not oversell it.

**(3) Scaffold-side K(z) conditioning for the DR plant family.** No direct precedent in this cluster — all three papers are single-plant works with no notion of a family of plants indexed by `z`. The closest partial analog is [78]'s episode-indexed confidence-weighted blending, which could be reinterpreted as a pattern for combining a slowly-refined K(z) with priors *if* K(z) were updated across sequential rollouts rather than within one — but this is a stretch, not a demonstrated mechanism. This is a genuine gap in the trio; family-conditioned/hierarchical Koopman precedent, if it exists, would need to come from other survey references, not [78]/[84]/[86].
