## SAFETY / CONSTRAINTS in Koopman Lifted Space — Research Findings

### 1. Koopman-lifted Control Barrier Functions (CBF-QP safety filters)

**Foundational: Data-Driven Safety-Critical Control: Synthesizing CBFs With Koopman Operators** — Folkestad, Chen, Ames, Burdick, *IEEE Control Systems Letters* 5(6):2012–2017, 2021 (DOI 10.1109/LCSYS.2020.3046159; IEEE Xplore doc 9483057).
- Mechanism: learns a finite Koopman/EDMD lifted model from data, then synthesizes a CBF whose safety condition is affine in the lifted coordinates, enforced via a QP that minimally perturbs a nominal controller. This is the paper the field cites as the origin of "Koopman-CBF."
- Evidence: I could confirm the paper exists (DOI, IEEE Xplore record, multiple independent citations describing quadrotor-class validation) but could not extract exact quantitative numbers — the abstract/PDF sat behind a paywall/cert error (`ames.caltech.edu` cert mismatch, researchr.org abstract missing). Flagging this explicitly rather than inventing numbers.
- Maturity: peer-reviewed, 2021, widely cited as the seed paper for this sub-line.

**Neural Koopman Control Barrier Functions for Safety-Critical Control of Unknown Nonlinear Systems** — Zinage, Bakolas, arXiv:2209.07685 (2022 ACC).
- Mechanism: lifts the unknown nonlinear system into a higher-dimensional **bilinear** (not purely linear) Koopman system via a learner/falsifier pair that jointly learns the Koopman-bilinear model and a CBF for it. Proves an ℓ2-error bound between the bilinear surrogate and the true nonlinear system, tunable via the Lipschitz constant of the chosen observables, so the learned CBF is provably a valid CBF for the *original* unknown system, not just the surrogate. QP built from this CBF gives the safe input.
- Evidence: numerical simulations only (no hardware); exact task/metrics not extractable from available excerpts — flagged as unverified beyond the mechanism claim.
- Maturity: peer-reviewed (ACC 2023), still simulation-only.

**Robust Koopman Control Barrier Filters for Safe Actor-Critic Reinforcement Learning** — Kushwaha, Biron, arXiv:2605.26452 (submitted May 2026, revised June 2026).
- Mechanism: this is the paper most directly on-point for your question — it wires a Koopman-CBF safety filter directly onto an RL actor-critic loop. Learns a finite-dim Koopman predictor from data → lifts state trajectories to observables → CBF constraints become affine inequalities in lifted space → QP safety layer projects the raw SAC action onto the safe set. Handles finite-dimensional EDMD approximation error by **tightening the CBF condition with a "projected residual margin" estimated from held-out rollout data** (a data-driven robustness margin, not an analytic bound). The critic trains on the *executed* (filtered) action; the actor is regularized toward the Koopman-CBF feasible set so the policy learns to need the filter less over time.
- Evidence: CartPole stabilization/tracking — zero constraint violations while matching unconstrained-SAC returns. Safety Gymnasium (high-dim locomotion) — violations reduced in some settings but not uniformly; authors themselves flag that first-order velocity barriers and linear EDMD models are insufficient there, motivating higher-order/multi-step Koopman-CBF extensions. No QP-rate/embedded-timing numbers reported in the fetched content.
- Maturity: very recent preprint (2026), not yet a venue publication; explicitly reports partial failure on the harder benchmark class (locomotion), which is a data point about limits.

### 2. Whole-body safety on real manipulator hardware (closest analog to a UUV-manipulator)

**Whole-Body Safe Control of Robotic Systems with Koopman Neural Dynamics** — Jung, Abuduweili, Li, Liu (CMU Robotics Institute), arXiv:2603.03740v3, March 2026.
- Mechanism: neural embedding ψ_ω lifts state to z_k = [x_k; ψ_ω(x_k)], with linear Koopman dynamics z_{k+1}=A z_k + B u_k learned end-to-end via K-step prediction loss. Safety is a signed-distance barrier ϕ_0(x) whose time-derivative is made **linear in u** through the lifted model, so the whole safety constraint drops directly into a single QP (no separate safety-filter step — the KMPC solves tracking + safety jointly). An adversarial min-max fine-tuning step specifically targets QP infeasibility at the boundary of the safe set (reshapes a safety-index exponent/margin) rather than leaving that failure mode unaddressed.
- Evidence (concrete numbers, fetched directly): tested on a **Kinova Gen3 7-DoF arm** (fixed-base manipulator, structurally the closest published analog to your arm-DOF) and a Unitree Go2 quadruped. 3D multi-link case: KMPC cumulative cost 13,154 vs NMPC-10's 81,261, average max safety-index violation ϕ = 0.01496. Adversarial fine-tuning cut infeasible QP solves from 632/4000 to 113/4000 in multi-obstacle scenes. **QP solve time ≈ 0.0389 s/step ⇒ ~25 Hz**, reported as real-time and 4.2× faster than the NMPC baseline. Sim-to-real fine-tuning brought hardware prediction error down to 0.140 rad joint-angle / 0.031 m end-effector error.
- Maturity: 2026 preprint, but with real hardware (Kinova arm) results and honest reporting of a residual ~2.8% infeasibility rate after mitigation — the single most load-bearing data point for your feasibility question below.

### 3. Constraint tightening / robust MPC on Koopman-linear models

**Data-driven Predictive Tracking Control based on Koopman Operators (KTMPC)** — Wang, Yang, Pu, Manzie, arXiv:2208.12000 (2022, revised 2023) — this is the exact "Wang et al." paper named in your brief.
- Mechanism: builds a Koopman-lifted linear model for tracking MPC, then applies an **iterative robust constraint-tightening scheme** to absorb the mismatch between the true nonlinear plant and the finite-dim Koopman surrogate — the tightening is computed offline/iteratively from bounds on the modeling error, then baked into the MPC's state/input constraint sets so the online QP never has to reason about the nonlinear error term directly. Proves recursive feasibility and input-to-state stability (ISS) to a neighborhood of the true optimal reachable steady output, under mild assumptions and bounded model error.
- Evidence: validated on a numerical example and an autonomous ground-vehicle reference-tracking case; exact quantitative tracking-error/violation numbers weren't extractable from the fetched abstract-level content (flagged as unverified detail; the theoretical guarantees themselves, recursive feasibility + ISS, are confirmed from the text).
- Maturity: peer-reviewed line of work (also has journal/conference variants), theoretically the most rigorous of the group — this is the right citation if you want a provable constraint-satisfaction guarantee rather than an empirical CBF margin.

### 4. Reachability analysis in Koopman-lifted linear space

**Scalable Data-Driven Reachability Analysis and Control via Koopman Operators with Conformal Coverage Guarantees** — Nath, Yin, Chou, arXiv:2601.01076 (Jan 2026, under review).
- Mechanism: NN Koopman lifting function → closed-loop reachable sets computed cheaply in the lifted *linear* space (reachability of a linear system is far cheaper than for the true nonlinear plant) → sets mapped back to state space via NN-verification tooling. Crucially, it doesn't rely on an analytic Lipschitz-style error bound like Folkestad/Zinage — it uses **conformal prediction** to produce a statistically-valid inflation of the reachable set (coverage at a user-specified probability), and these bounds are built to generalize across different reference trajectories without recomputation per reference.
- Evidence: tested on 11-D Hopper, 28-D Swimmer (MuJoCo), and 12-D quadcopter — dimensionality in the same ballpark as your 72D obs / 28D p_t stack, though these are simulated benchmark systems, not a physical vehicle. Reported gains in coverage rate, compute efficiency, and reduced conservativeness vs. prior Koopman-reachability baselines (exact numeric deltas not extracted from the fetched summary).
- Maturity: 2026 preprint, simulation-only, no real-time/embedded timing reported.

## Applicability to Your Stack

**Where it would plug in.** Every one of these methods is a **deployment-time (or training-time) safety wrapper around the action**, not a replacement for training-time constraint handling — so it's additive to IPO, not competing with it. The natural insertion point is exactly what your brief frames: a thin layer between the student GRU's action output and the real UUV thrusters/arm, running on the vehicle's onboard compute at ≤25 Hz. Two live options: (a) Kushwaha/Biron's Koopman-CBF-QP pattern applied post-hoc to your trained policy's action, using rollout data from your (extensive) sim DR distribution to fit the Koopman predictor and the residual margin; (b) Wang et al.'s constraint-tightened Koopman-tracking-MPC as a short-horizon safety-MPC wrapper, which buys you a provable (ISS/recursive-feasibility) guarantee rather than an empirical margin — more defensible for a real vehicle but needs an explicit Koopman model of your 6-DOF UUV+arm plant, which you don't currently have (your encoder is a physical-parameter→latent map for the *critic*, not a forward dynamics model usable for a lifted-linear predictor).

**Blockers.**
- **QP rate vs. bus rate**: the one directly-measured real-time number in this whole set — Jung et al.'s Kinova-arm KMPC at ~0.0389 s/step (~25 Hz) — lands *exactly* at your obs-bus ceiling, on a desktop-class solve for a 7-DOF arm alone, not a coupled 6-DOF vehicle + 2-DOF arm system with thruster-fault dynamics. That's a caution flag, not a green light: your embedded/onboard board is very unlikely to match the compute used in that paper, and a coupled vehicle+arm Koopman lift will need a materially larger observable dictionary than a fixed-base arm, which grows the QP.
- **Residual infeasibility isn't zero anywhere**: even after adversarial tuning, Jung et al. report 113/4000 (~2.8%) infeasible QP solves; Kushwaha/Biron's CBF filter degrades on the harder (locomotion-class) benchmark. A safety filter that can itself go infeasible under fault/DR conditions is a new failure mode to engineer around (fallback controller, slack variables), not a strict safety upgrade for free.
- **Model validity under faults**: your DR distribution includes thruster faults and hydrodynamic/payload variation specifically because a *single* linear (even lifted) model doesn't stay valid across that range. None of the found papers demonstrate Koopman-CBF/MPC safety filters validated under actuator-fault-scale distribution shift — Wang et al.'s constraint-tightening is designed for bounded model mismatch, and it's an open question whether your fault magnitudes stay inside that bound; this would need to be checked empirically, not assumed.
- **No existing forward-dynamics Koopman model in your stack**: unlike the encoder audit you've already closed off (no reconstruction/auxiliary losses on p_t→z), a genuine Koopman *predictor* for a safety filter is a different artifact — a forward-simulatable lifted linear model of your full plant — which none of your current architecture provides; building and validating one is nontrivial extra engineering, separate from the RL/encoder work.

**Verdict: STRETCH** — a Koopman-lifted CBF-QP or constraint-tightened MPC safety filter is a real, actively-published technique (2021–2026, several 2026 preprints) with the closest published analog (Jung et al., real Kinova-arm hardware) sitting right at your bus-rate ceiling for a *simpler* system than yours; it's plausible as a future deployment-time safety layer but requires building a validated forward Koopman model of the coupled vehicle+arm+fault dynamics and confirming QP solve time on your actual embedded board before it's a real option — not something to fold into the current teacher/student campaign.
