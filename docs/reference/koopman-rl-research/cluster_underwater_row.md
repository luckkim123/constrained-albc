## Bottom Line

The underwater cluster does **not** support a bare affine-in-raw-action Koopman form, but it also does **not** demonstrate that bilinear/control-coherent/switched forms are required — it demonstrates something narrower and more actionable: **affine-in-a-well-chosen-nonlinear-action-encoding can work**, but only when (a) that encoding is expressive enough to cancel the actuator's own nonlinearity, and (b) the linear operator pair is allowed to adapt online rather than staying frozen across disturbance/DR regimes. None of the five references combine actuator nonlinearity + latency + fault-like mode switching simultaneously the way ALBC does, so the cluster leaves the fault-DR (switched-dynamics) question genuinely open — which matches the survey's own explicit statement that Koopman theory "for systems with inputs and systems whose evolution is governed by broader hybrid dynamics remains open" (Sec. VII, Conclusion, PDF p.17).

## 2. Per-paper deep read

### [90] Mamakoukas et al., "Derivative-Based Koopman Operators..." — deepest, most relevant source (full text read)

**Mechanism.** Populates the Koopman dictionary Ψ(s) with the state **and its higher-order time derivatives**, not arbitrary basis functions (Sec. III). For control, it uses the explicit **control-affine form** Ψ_s(s_{k+1}) = AΨ_s(s_k) + Bu_k (Eq. 9–10) — i.e. affine in the *raw* control signal u, obtained by a closed-form least-squares fit (Eq. 5–6). This is the plainest possible instance of Sec. II-C.2's "affine input form."

**Assumptions.** Dynamics continuously differentiable up to order n; derivatives either analytically known (structured case) or numerically estimated from noisy measurements (fully data-driven case, central finite differences + moving-average filtering).

**Error analysis/guarantees.** The paper's central theoretical contribution: Theorem 1 gives a **provable Taylor-remainder error bound** |e_k| ≤ Tⁿ⁺¹/(n+1)! · |f_max^(n+1)| for propagating n derivatives over horizon T (Eq. 22) — the first such bound for a general-nonlinear-system Koopman representation (their claim, and consistent with how the survey cites this line of work). The bound is derivable even when the operator is learned from noisy data (Sec. III-B, Eq. 29), and Fig. 3–4 empirically validate it stays a valid, if conservative, envelope under high measurement noise.

**Evidence with numbers.**
- Pendulum-MPC benchmark: Koopman-MPC cost 19.71 (10 s) vs. NARX +12.43%, SINDy +18.32%, plain-linear +30.61%; Koopman-LQR only +0.97% worse than Koopman-MPC.
- Robotic-fish LQR-tracking simulation (figure-8): Koopman-LQR cost 3.35 vs. 95% higher error for a plain linear model with the *same* state set.
- **Hardware** (Michigan State Smart Microsystems Lab tail-actuated fish, Fig. 8): 72 real runs × 36 actuation combos; Koopman-LQR beats a tuned PID on velocity/angle tracking in essentially every line/circle-trajectory condition (Fig. 12), and **online model updating significantly outperforms the offline-trained model specifically when unmodeled fluid disturbance (propeller-generated ripples) is present** (Fig. 12, "waves" columns) — direct hardware evidence that a *static* affine (A,B) pair degrades under real disturbance and needs online re-fitting to hold up.

**Actuator nonlinearity → the single most relevant finding.** The physical actuation is tail amplitude/bias (α_a, α_o); the raw dynamics are *not* affine in these. The authors derive a hand-crafted nonlinear reparametrization u₁ = α_a²(3/2 − 3/2α_o² − 3/8α_a²), u₂ = α_a²α_o (Eq. 37) specifically because it makes the dynamics affine in (u₁,u₂) — this is exactly the φ_u(a_t) "nonlinear action encoder" idea in KIPPO, done **by hand from a known parametric drag/thrust ODE (Eq. 35–36, Fossen-style)**, not learned. They then invert (u₁,u₂) back to (α_a,α_o) via constrained SQP before each control update (Eq. 38).

**Hydrodynamic effects captured vs. missed.** Captured: quadratic/signed-quadratic drag v|v|, an arctan(v_y/v_x)-type angle-of-attack proxy, added-mass scaling (m_ax, m_ay) and yaw added-inertia (J_az) as *coefficients* inside a known functional form, and sgn(ω)ω² Coriolis-like coupling (Eq. 36). Missed/limiting per the authors' own Discussion (Sec. V, PDF p.15): the "average model" is admittedly inaccurate at low forward speed (it predicts zero bias-induced rotation when amplitude=0, which is physically wrong once the vehicle already has forward velocity); they explicitly suggest **Kirchhoff's rigid-body-in-fluid equations** as the fix, and note wall-collision disturbances in the confined tank degrade the model "temporarily."

**Maturity.** Journal (T-RO), theory + simulation + real hardware with statistically aggregated trials (10 trials × 2 trajectory types × 2 flow conditions) and error bars — the most mature entry in this cluster.

**Implication for our question.** Strong, direct precedent that (i) plain affine-in-*raw*-action fails for a nonlinear thruster-like actuator, (ii) a hand-derived nonlinear φ_u *can* restore affine structure — validating the KIPPO premise in principle — but (iii) achieving that required exploiting a *known* parametric form of the actuator nonlinearity, and (iv) even after doing so, robustness under real unmodeled disturbance required **online adaptation of (A,B)**, not a frozen affine model. Nothing here tests fault-like discrete mode changes.

### [150] Bevanda et al., "Towards Data-driven LQR with Koopmanizing Flows" — full text read

**Mechanism.** Learns a diffeomorphism d(x) (via composed affine-coupling-flow invertible NNs, 7 coupling layers, monomial-lift order p̄=10, D=65) mapping the *state* into Koopman-invariant coordinates, concurrently with a **linear-in-raw-control** term: ż = Az + Bu (Eq. 8b/21), i.e. the standard affine input form applied to a *learned, expressive* state lift but a *fixed linear* action channel. Diffeomorphism + control learned jointly (their point of departure from prior two-stage Koopmanizing-Flows work) so the LQR-optimal policy is u = −Kψ(x) in closed form.

**Assumptions.** Control enters the *original* ODE linearly (ẋ = a(x) + Bu, Assumption in Sec. 2.1) — i.e. the paper explicitly assumes what our system does *not* have (our actuation is not linear-entry at the physical level; it goes through a nonlinear ESC+thrust map). They flag this themselves.

**Explicit limitation, verbatim (Remark 1):** *"If the actuation entry in (1) was nonlinear, assuming an LTI controlled system in the lifted model becomes only locally accurate... but still can suffice for short-horizon prediction and control."* This is the clearest single statement in the whole cluster on the affine-vs-nonlinear-actuation tradeoff, and it is a **caveat against**, not evidence for, unconditional affine adequacy.

**Underwater relevance.** Example 1 (Sec. 5) is explicitly built from *"characteristic hydrodynamic damping of underwater vehicles (Fossen, 2011)"*: ẋ₂ = −x₁ − x₂ − x₂|x₂| + u — i.e. the *only* nonlinearity is a state-side quadratic-drag term v|v|, with control entering perfectly linearly (an idealized, non-actuator-nonlinear underwater example). This is why Sec. IV-E cites [150] for "linear constraints in model-based controllers" — the LQR/quadratic-cost machinery, not any actuation-nonlinearity treatment.

**Evidence with numbers.** Simulation only (no hardware). Example 1 (Fossen-damping system): KFc-LQR cuts accumulated L2 tracking error by **97.06%** vs. linear-model LQR. Example 2 (position- and velocity-dependent damping): 80.34% reduction vs. gEDMDc-LQR at matched lift dimension D=65, attributed to learning the lift and (A,B) *jointly* rather than in the two-stage manner of Korda & Mezić (2020).

**Maturity.** Workshop/conference paper (IFAC ICONS), theory + 2 low-dimensional simulation examples, no hardware, no underwater-specific actuator model.

**Implication for our question.** [150] is explicit that affine-in-raw-control is only *locally* valid once actuation is nonlinear, and names **"extending the model capacity to systems with state-dependent actuation"** as unaddressed future work (Sec. 6) — i.e. the authors themselves flag the bilinear-like extension as the open next step, which is exactly our design fork.

### [87] Lin, Liu, Liu, Wang, "Dynamic Modeling of Robotic Fish considering Background Flow using Koopman Operators" (FARM) — full text read

**Mechanism.** State x_k=[p_x,p_y,θ,v_x,v_y,ω]ᵀ (6D), control u_k=[α_a,α_o,α_f]ᵀ (raw tail amplitude/bias/frequency, 3D), and — the paper's actual novelty — an explicit **disturbance/current channel** ρ_k=[ρ_x,ρ_y]ᵀ (local mean background-flow velocity, 2D) all enter the **same joint lift**: Ψ(x,u,ρ)=[xᵀ,uᵀ,ρᵀ, ψ₁,...,ψ₈₉]ᵀ (Eq. 6), with the 89 extra basis functions built as **pairwise multiplicative combinations selecting any two elements from {x,u,ρ}** (Eq. 13, ψ₁₋₆₅) plus sin/cos of combinations (ψ₆₆₋₈₉), total lift dim 100. This is Sec. II-C.1's "joint lifting" category extended to a third variable (disturbance), not the affine-input-form Table I's coarse tick suggests — **worth flagging as a categorization simplification we resolved by reading the primary source**. Critically, because x_i·u_j cross-products are explicitly included as basis functions inside the *same* propagated vector, this is functionally a **bilinear coupling between state and control, re-expressed as linear-in-the-lift** — a concrete, working precedent for a bilinear-flavored aux model, achieved purely through dictionary design rather than an explicit bilinear operator.

**Assumptions.** Planar locomotion only (3-DOF, not full 6-DOF); local flow field summarized as one mean-velocity vector within a 0.5 m radius of the fish (matched to fish body size); flow field itself obtained from **COMSOL simulation, not direct measurement** — an explicitly acknowledged sim-to-real gap in the disturbance input itself.

**Error analysis/guarantees.** None (pure least-squares fit, Eq. 9–11); no theoretical bound — contrast with [90].

**Evidence with numbers (real hardware).** MAgIC Lab (ShanghaiTech) tail-actuated fish, 0.51 m/1.046 kg, in a 4 m×4 m×0.5 m pump-array flow tank; 2000 training trajectories at flow speeds {0, 0.27, 0.38} m/s; tested (held out) at those speeds **and** an unseen 0.46 m/s. 60 s (300-step) long-horizon RMSE, Table I: at 0.46 m/s flow, a flow-agnostic baseline degrades to θ-RMSE 0.92 rad / v_x-RMSE 0.117 m/s vs. FARM's 0.52 rad / 0.083 m/s — baseline error grows ≈4.8× from static to 0.46 m/s while FARM's grows only ≈2.7×. Interesting counter-evidence: **at zero flow, FARM is slightly worse than the flow-agnostic baseline** (x-RMSE 0.25 vs. 0.13 m) — attributed to the noisy (COMSOL-simulated, not measured) flow-velocity input acting as an extra noise channel when the true disturbance is near zero.

**Maturity.** Conference (IROS 2024), real hardware with held-out unseen-disturbance generalization test — solid but narrower in scope (planar, single disturbance type) than [90].

**Implication for our question.** Two direct, quantitatively-grounded lessons: (1) folding a DR-relevant disturbance variable into the lift as **multiplicative cross-terms with state/action** (not merely as an appended raw feature or additive bias) measurably improves robustness to that disturbance — directly transferable to how ocean-current terms are represented in the ALBC obs/dictionary. (2) A DR-relevant feature that is itself noisy/estimated (not ground-truth) can **cost you accuracy exactly at the operating point where that disturbance is absent** — a bias/variance caution for how much fault/DR-conditioning to push into φ_x versus leaving it to a separate mechanism.

### [88] Rodwell, Buzhardt, Tallapragada, "A Koopman operator approach for the pitch stabilization of a hydrofoil in an unsteady flow field" — **abstract/secondary-source level only; full text paywalled, not independently verified beyond converging search-engine summaries**

**Mechanism (as reported by 3 independent search summaries, consistent across all of them).** Builds the Koopman operator from **time-series pressure measurements** on the hydrofoil surface plus angular velocity — i.e. a Hankel/time-delay-style, sensor-driven dictionary (survey's HVOK category, Sec. II-B.2), not a hand-picked polynomial dictionary. The actuator is an **internal reaction wheel with an integral saturation constraint**; the paper is reported to develop "a linear representation for both the complex fluid-structure interaction as well as actuation effects" and to "formulate constrained optimal control problems solved with model predictive control." One summary states Koopman-derived features outperformed CNN-based feature extraction for position estimation.

**Assumptions/data/task.** Simulated unsteady free-stream flow (per the reported abstract), pitch-stabilization task via MPC with the reaction-wheel's integral-saturation constraint enforced at the controller layer.

**Maturity.** Conference (ACC 2023); simulation-based per available summaries — cannot confirm hardware without the full text.

**Implication for our question.** If accurately reported, the actuator **nonlinearity here (saturation) is handled as an MPC constraint on top of a linear/Koopman predictor**, rather than by making the lifted dynamics themselves capture the saturation. That is a genuinely different architectural lever than φ_u-encoding — pushing actuator nonlinearity into the controller's constraint set instead of the dynamics model — but it is less directly transferable to our setting, where the aux model sits inside an RL actor's auxiliary prediction loss rather than an MPC constraint set. **Flag:** this entire paragraph rests on abstract-level secondary summaries, not the primary text; treat mechanism claims here as lower-confidence than [87]/[90]/[150].

### [89] Li, Xu, Liu, Xu, "Learning-based Extended Dynamic Mode Decomposition for Addressing Path-following Problem of Underactuated Ships with Unknown Dynamics" — **abstract/secondary-source level only; full text paywalled (Springer, no OA location), not independently verified beyond converging search-engine summaries**

**Mechanism (as reported, consistent across ≥4 independent search summaries).** Learns **Koopman eigenfunctions via supervised learning** directly from experimental data (rather than a hand-picked dictionary), uses these as EDMD lifting functions to build a linear lifted state-space model, and drives an MPC controller with a **line-of-sight (LOS) guidance law** for path-following — explicitly targeting ships with **unknown hydrodynamic coefficients and unknown ship parameters** (i.e., the same class of problem as our hydro-coefficient DR, generalized from "unknown-but-fixed" to plausibly "varying").

**Assumptions/task.** Underactuated surface vessel (fewer control DOF than state DOF, analogous structurally to our 6-DOF-state/8-DOF-action but different direction of under/over-actuation); path-following via LOS+MPC.

**Maturity.** Journal (IJCAS 2022); data-driven, experiment-derived eigenfunctions per the abstract, but data volume, exact input-handling form (affine vs. joint-lifted), and numeric tracking-error results are **not verifiable from the accessible summaries** — flagged explicitly.

**Implication for our question.** Weak positive precedent that a **learned (not hand-designed) dictionary can generalize across unknown/varying hydrodynamic coefficients** in an underwater/marine setting when combined with MPC — supportive of the general viability of learned φ_x under hydro-parameter DR, but **cannot confirm** whether their input handling is affine-in-raw-control or something richer, so it should not be cited as evidence either for or against the affine-vs-bilinear fork specifically.

## 3. Synthesis — direct answer to the design question

**Is affine(-in-encoded-action) adequate for our actuator nonlinearity + fault DR?** The cluster's strongest, fully-verified evidence ([90]) says: affine-in-*raw* action is inadequate (confirmed by the authors needing a hand-derived nonlinear reparametrization to restore affine structure), affine-in-*well-chosen-encoded* action **can** work but the encoding they used was hand-derived from known physics, not learned end-to-end, and even then the linear operator pair needed **online re-fitting** to survive real unmodeled disturbance (their own hardware result: offline-trained Koopman-LQR degrades under fluid disturbance; online-updated Koopman-LQR recovers). [150] independently and explicitly names nonlinear/state-dependent actuation as the point where affine-in-raw-control degrades to "locally accurate" and lists it as their own unaddressed future work. Neither paper tests anything resembling discrete fault-mode switching.

**Do the cited works argue for bilinear/control-coherent/switched forms specifically?** Not explicitly — none of the five implements a bilinear-operator Koopman model, a control-coherent embedding, or a switched/multi-mode Koopman model for underwater actuation. But [87] shows a *de facto* bilinear mechanism achieved purely by dictionary design: including state×action (and here, ×disturbance) multiplicative cross-terms inside a single jointly-propagated lift, which is linear-in-the-lift but bilinear-in-the-original-coordinates. That is a concrete, hardware-validated existence proof that the **bilinear extension is implementable via dictionary cross-terms rather than requiring a structurally different (bilinear-operator) model class** — a lower-cost way to hedge against affine's limits than a full switched-system redesign.

**Net recommendation implied by the evidence, not asserted beyond it:** treat pure static affine-in-φ_u(a_t) as under-supported by this cluster for the combined ESC-filter + nonlinear-thrust + latency + fault-DR stack. The evidence more specifically supports (a) investing in an expressive, ideally physics-seeded φ_u that targets the *known* approximate structure of the thrust nonlinearity (quadratic-with-deadband, per your task description) the way [90] seeded u₁,u₂ from a known cubic drag/thrust ODE, (b) adding state×action cross-terms in φ_x/the aux model (à la [87]) as a cheap bilinear hedge rather than a full switched-system redesign, and (c) not assuming a single static (K,B) suffices across the full DR/fault range — [90]'s hardware result argues for some form of within-episode or regime-conditioned adaptation, which for fault DR specifically is unprecedented in this cluster and is exactly the gap the survey itself calls "open" for input+hybrid dynamics.

## 4. Reusable dictionary-design hints for a 6-DOF UUV under hydrodynamic DR

- **Signed-quadratic velocity terms (v|v|) per DOF** appear independently in both physics-informed underwater examples in this cluster ([90]'s f₁,f₂ drag terms; [150]'s Fossen-style Example 1) — the single most consistently-recurring "observable a marine practitioner found necessary."
- **Angle-of-attack-like ratio terms** (arctan(v_y/v_x) in [90]) show up as an interpretable proxy for lift/drag direction, not just velocity magnitude — worth checking whether your 69–72D obs already encodes a directional (not just magnitude) hydrodynamic cue per DOF.
- **Added-mass / added-inertia effects** are treated by both physics-informed papers as *coefficients inside a known functional form* (m_ax, m_ay, J_az in [90]) rather than as separate learned observables — suggests added-mass DR is more naturally absorbed as a parameter the encoder must infer (via z) than as an explicit extra input feature, unless you deliberately want it as a directly-supervised auxiliary target.
- **Current/flow-relative velocity should enter as multiplicative cross-terms with state, not just as an additive/EMA bias channel** — [87]'s hardware result (baseline degrades ~4.8× vs. FARM's ~2.7× from static to 0.46 m/s flow) is the cluster's clearest quantitative case for this, and directly informs how your existing bias-EMA current term could be extended.
- **A noisy/estimated DR-relevant input can cost accuracy at the disturbance-absent operating point** ([87]'s zero-flow regression) — budget for this bias/variance tradeoff if fault- or current-indicator features are only approximately known at train time.
- **Actuator-nonlinearity handling has two documented architectural options in this cluster**: (i) cancel it via a physics-seeded nonlinear encoding of the action so the lifted dynamics stay affine ([90]'s u₁,u₂), or (ii) push it into the controller as a constraint on top of a linear/Koopman predictor rather than into the dynamics model itself (reported for [88], lower confidence). Only (i) is directly applicable to an RL-actor auxiliary-loss setting like KIPPO.
