# Axis B: Partial / Structured Lifting

## B1. Precedent for partitioned/structured Koopman dictionaries

**Selby & Asada, "Learning of Causal Observable Functions for Koopman-DFL Lifting Linearization" (arXiv:2104.02004, IEEE RA-L 2021).** Mechanism: Dual-Faceted Linearization (DFL) keeps physical **primary states** x (position/velocity-type, integral causality) unlifted, and adds **secondary observables** η = g(x) only for the outputs of nonlinear elements (bond-graph effort/flow terms) — this is explicitly partial/structured lifting, not full-state embedding. The paper's key contribution is a **causality filter**: an observable is only a valid lifting candidate if η = g(x) (state-only); anything of the form g(x,u) is "anti-causal" (it needs future control input to predict itself one step ahead) and is explicitly projected out via ζ*(x) = ζ(x,u) − D̂u. Evidence (excavator bucket-soil, AGX Dynamics terramechanics sim, integrated-squared-error on held-out trajectories): the causality-filtered model (L3) beats the same model without the filter by ~8×, beats a no-observable model by ~10×, beats full/naive high-dim Koopman (dim 67) by ~20×, and beats DMDc (dim 12) by ~5.6×. Maturity: **simulation only** (AGX terramechanics; toy spring-damper), no real excavator hardware; authors note out-of-distribution initial conditions degrade performance substantially.

**Köhler et al., "Koopman Form of Nonlinear Systems with Inputs" (arXiv:2207.12132).** Mechanism: proves that if only the state x is lifted through Φ (inputs left raw), the resulting Koopman-with-control form is necessarily Φ(x⁺) = AΦ(x) + B(x,u)u — i.e. an **LPV, state-dependent input matrix**, not a fixed affine-in-u form as commonly assumed (EDMDc). This is directly a "the affine-input form is itself partial lifting" result the task brief anticipated: the paper explicitly justifies keeping u raw/unlifted ("we use a construction based on only state-dependent observables... to avoid ending up with a Koopman model without explicit external inputs") because input-lifted observables break standard LQR/MPC control-design machinery. No discussion of lifting only a *sub-block* of the state itself. Maturity: theoretical, rigorous proofs, illustrative low-dimensional examples only — no large-scale validation.

**Lazar, "From Product Hilbert Spaces to the Generalized Koopman Operator and the Nonlinear Fundamental Lemma" (arXiv:2508.07494)**, applied in **"Koopman Operator Learning for Predictive Control via Khatri-Rao Kernel Regression" (arXiv:2606.02938).** Mechanism: GeKo lifts state and input through **independent dictionaries** Ψ_x, Ψ_u and forms a bilinear model z⁺ = K(z⊗v) — structurally the cleanest precedent for "lift some channels, treat others differently," though it partitions state-vs-input rather than state-vs-state. GeKo is proved to strictly generalize both linear-in-control and bilinear Koopman forms. Evidence: validated on the Lorenz system (chaotic benchmark), stabilizes an unstable equilibrium from varied initial conditions, 101ms mean per-step MPC solve — a proof-of-concept, not a real system. No head-to-head numbers against joint (non-partitioned) lifting are given.

**SE(3)-structured Koopman dictionaries for quadrotors (arXiv:2103.03363).** Mechanism: observables are derived to respect the SE(3)/SO(3) manifold geometry of rotation R, angular velocity ω, position p, velocity v (avoiding Euler-angle singularities), while thrust/moment control inputs are treated as passthrough terms giving an affine-in-control model — geometric partial lifting where the *lifted block* is chosen by physical symmetry group, not by trial. Convergence of the observable sequence is proven pointwise. Evidence given is approximation/observable-prediction error against a non-geometric baseline; no explicit closed-loop tracking-error table was recoverable from the fetched text. Maturity: **simulation only** (a related SE(3) Koopman-MPC paper, Chen & Chirarattananon, ScienceDirect S2405896323024242, was found but is paywalled — 403 on fetch — so its hardware-flight claims are **unverified**, flagged, not asserted).

## B2. Does partial lifting of a *learned policy's* input have precedent/evidence?

This is the weak link. **"Koopman Operators in Robot Learning" survey (arXiv:2408.04200)** was fetched specifically to check for this pattern and reports: no discussion of partial/structured lifting of policy observation channels, no formal taxonomy for "what to lift vs pass through" in robot-learning Koopman work (it only categorizes *how* dictionaries are built — manual / physics-informed / neural — not *which subset* of a state gets a dictionary), no ablations of partial-vs-full lifting, and no mention of SE(3)/geometric structured dictionaries as a named category. This is itself informative: **the survey confirms the specific question (partial lifting of a subset of an RL policy's obs vector) is an open gap**, not settled practice.

The closest *quantitative* analog found is **"Autoencoder-Based and Physically Motivated Koopman Lifted States for Wind Farm MPC" (arXiv:2409.06523)** — physics-motivated (structured, hand-selected, partial) lifting vs learned autoencoder lifting, for MPC (not RL). Results are genuinely mixed, not a clean win for structured lifting:

| Lifting | Scenario 1 (model matched) TE | Scenario 2 (0.003% Cp degradation) TE |
|---|---|---|
| K24 physics-motivated (24 hand-crafted states) | 21.6 kW | 120.7 kW (severe degradation) |
| AE1 (24 learned states) | 17.3 kW (20% better) | 87.6 kW (27% better than physics) |
| AE2 (2 learned states) | 66.9 kW (3× worse) | 66.9 kW (45% better than physics) |

Takeaway: physics-structured lifting was *more accurate under nominal conditions* than a tiny 2-state autoencoder, but *lost to both autoencoders once the physical model assumption drifted* — structured lifting is only as good as the physics it encodes, and degrades ungracefully outside that regime. Simulation-only (WFSim), no field data.

**RoboKoop (arXiv:2409.03107, CoRL 2024)** was checked as a candidate learned-policy example but its PDF resisted extraction; the abstract only establishes it lifts *visual* features into a Koopman-linear latent for a linear controller — whether proprioception/commands are passed through unlifted could not be confirmed from available text. **Flagged as unverified**, not used as evidence either way.

## B3. Applicability to our stack

**Proposed design**: φ_x lifts only the 20D current-proprio block (or a narrower 9D attitude/rate core) of the 72D obs; the 3D command, 3D leaky-integral error, 3D bias-EMA, and 46D history (30D tracking + 16D action) pass through identity into y_t.

Three lines of precedent support the partition, each mapping onto a different excluded block:

- **Commands (3D) → exogenous input, not state.** Directly matches the Köhler et al. affine-input result: our attitude/yaw-rate command is functionally u, not x — standard practice keeps u raw. Strong, direct fit.
- **Integral error (3D) + bias-EMA (3D) → already-known-linear controller-internal states.** A leaky integrator and an EMA are first-order linear ODEs by construction — under Selby & Asada's causal-observable logic these are exactly the kind of "integral causality, primary-state" quantities that don't belong in the *lifted* set (DFL keeps such states as primary x, unlifted); lifting something already Koopman-linear wastes model capacity and needlessly widens the aux-loss surface.
- **History (46D) → already a delay embedding.** The 30D tracking history (stride-3, 3 past steps) and 16D action history are themselves a hand-built Hankel-style time-delay lift — a well-known way Koopman methods recover hidden state. Re-lifting an already-lifted block through φ_x a second time is redundant, not partial-lifting-for-a-reason.

One real mismatch worth naming: Selby & Asada's DFL doesn't lift raw *states*, it adds a *secondary* nonlinear-observable block alongside primary states left untouched. Our φ_x proposal instead *transforms* the 20D block itself into y_t (representation-learning framing, closer to SKooP/KOAP than to DFL). The causality argument for excluding integral/bias/commands still holds, but "DFL says lift the proprio block" is not literally what DFL says — that's an extrapolation, not a direct precedent, and should be represented as such rather than cited as settled.

**Stack-specific blocker**: narrowing further to the 9D attitude/rate core (dropping the 5D arm + 6D thruster-ESC state from the lifted block) risks excluding exactly the nonlinear couplings — thruster fault DR, arm-base coupling — that dominate this stack's DR curriculum (per CLAUDE.md's hydro/payload/thruster-fault DR list) and that a Koopman lift is supposed to linearize. If those are left in the identity path, φ_x may buy little beyond what a plain MLP would learn on the attitude subspace alone. This needs to be an explicit ablation (20D block vs 9D core), not assumed.

**Second blocker**: the wind-farm evidence (B2) shows structured/partial lifting is not uniformly better than full learned lifting — it's more interpretable and can be more accurate under matched conditions but degrades faster under distribution shift (our exact operating regime, given heavy DR). This tempers any expectation that partial lifting is a free robustness win.

**Third blocker**: no cited work validates this pattern in an RL actor-obs setting with aux losses decoupled from the RL loss — this would be a genuinely novel combination for this project, not literature reuse, and carries the same collapse risk this codebase already burned on the p_t→z encoder (reconstruction aux losses driving representation collapse) even though φ_x's target is a different tensor.

**Verdict: STRETCH.** The channel-partition principle (commands as u, integral/bias-EMA as already-linear, history as already-delay-embedded) is well-grounded in Koopman-with-control theory and DFL causality reasoning, and the 20D-vs-9D ablation is cheap to run. But no work — not even the robot-learning survey — has validated partial lifting of a subset of a policy's observation vector empirically, the closest quantitative analog (wind farm) shows mixed results under distribution shift (which describes this stack's DR regime), and the DFL precedent requires reframing (secondary-observable-augmentation, not block-transformation) to actually apply. Treat as an experiment to run with a tight ablation (full-72D lift vs 20D-block vs 9D-core vs no-lift baseline) rather than a design decision the literature already answers.
