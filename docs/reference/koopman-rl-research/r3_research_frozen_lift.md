# Frozen-Pretrained Lifting φ_x for RL Policy Input — Novelty Assessment

**Key**: frozen_lift · **Scope**: narrow brief (Q1–Q3 below) for the ALBC Koopman-lifting doc §17–19 middle-rung arm (pretrain φ_x offline on logged + scripted-excitation rollouts, freeze, train RL policy on frozen lifted input).

## Q1 — Does published RL work use a FROZEN PRETRAINED dynamics-aware lifting/embedding as policy input (state-based)?

**Short answer: no clean state-based precedent found for exactly this pattern (pretrain-offline → freeze → RL-from-scratch on top). The closest matches are all either (a) visual, (b) frozen-after-joint-training rather than frozen-after-offline-pretraining, or (c) frozen for auxiliary/adaptation modules rather than the main policy input.**

**(a) Frozen VISUAL encoders — well established, analogy only.** ATC (Stooke et al., 2021, ICML — "Decoupling Representation Learning from Reinforcement Learning") pretrains a convolutional encoder with an augmented temporal-contrast (contrastive) objective on expert demonstration images, then freezes the encoder weights and trains the RL agent (DQN-style, Atari/DMControl) on top of the frozen features; it beats several prior unsupervised-pretraining baselines and exceeds the end-to-end RL reference on 3/4 tested Atari games with the encoder frozen. This is the field's best-known "pretrain-then-freeze" RL result, but it is entirely pixel/visual — same lineage as PVR/R3M-style frozen visual representations for robot learning. It does not touch dynamics-aware *state* embeddings and is not evidence for the state-based case; cite as methodological analogy only, not precedent.

**(b) State-based frozen dynamics embeddings — the actual question.** Two lines came closest but neither matches exactly:
- **DynE (Whitney et al., 2019, "Dynamics-aware Embeddings," arXiv:1908.09357)** learns state and action embeddings jointly with a forward-prediction objective, tested on low-dimensional continuous-control state (not just pixels). However, per the paper's own framing (abstract-level verification only — WebFetch could not extract training-loop detail beyond the abstract) the embeddings are learned **simultaneously** with policy training, not pretrained-offline-then-frozen-before-RL-starts. This is a joint multi-task scheme, not the frozen-lift pattern.
- **RMA (Kumar et al., 2021, "Rapid Motor Adaptation for Legged Robots," arXiv:2107.04034)** is the strongest structural analog, though for a different purpose. Phase 1 jointly RL-trains a base policy π and an "environment factor encoder" μ that maps privileged parameters → an extrinsics vector z (this is philosophically the same slot as your φ_x: a compact dynamics-relevant lift of extra state). Phase 2 **freezes** both π and μ and trains a separate adaptation module to regress z from proprioceptive history alone. The key structural difference from your arm: RMA freezes an embedding that was learned *jointly with the policy it will later feed* (not pretrained on a separate offline dataset before any RL happens), and the thing trained against the frozen target is a small history-regressor, not a full RL policy learning from scratch on top of the frozen features.
- **SKooP (arXiv:2607.11624)** and the Koopman-LQR robot-policy-learning line (Kth, koopman-learning.github.io) both learn the Koopman embedding **concurrently** with the policy/critic (the embedding is updated by RL-driven or auxiliary losses during policy training), which is the opposite of freezing.

**(c) Concurrent-vs-two-stage comparison exists and argues against your pattern.** Ji et al., 2022 ("Concurrent Training of a Control Policy and a State Estimator for Dynamic and Robust Legged Locomotion," arXiv:2202.05481, snippet/abstract-level verification) explicitly compares a **Sequential** baseline — phase 1 trains a policy with ground-truth state, phase 2 trains a state estimator against the frozen phase-1 policy — against their proposed **Concurrent** scheme where policy and estimator co-adapt. This is the only paper found that runs the head-to-head "freeze-then-train-on-top" vs. "co-train" ablation in a state-estimation-for-locomotion setting structurally close to yours. Full quantitative delta was not extracted (arXiv fetch/search only returned abstract-level framing, not tables) — **flag for a follow-up full-text/PDF pass if the magnitude of the Concurrent-vs-Sequential gap matters for the health-gate threshold.**

**Verdict on Q1: semi-novel.** The pretrain-offline → freeze → RL-from-scratch-on-φ_x pattern for a *state-based dynamics lift* has no clean single-paper precedent. The nearest neighbors (RMA's frozen extrinsics encoder, ATC's frozen visual encoder, Ji et al.'s sequential-vs-concurrent ablation) each supply one piece of the picture — freeze discipline, offline-pretrain discipline, or the direct head-to-head comparison — but none combines all three the way the proposed arm does. Frame it in the doc as "structurally adjacent to RMA's frozen-encoder discipline and ATC's frozen-pretrained-representation discipline, applied to a state-based Koopman-style lift, which — as far as this search found — has not been directly published."

## Q2 — Evidence on the staleness failure mode (frozen φ trained on early-policy distribution degrades on late-policy states)?

**Evidence exists, but indirectly — via RMA's own follow-up literature, not via a direct φ-staleness study.**

- The core mechanism is well documented as a general RL principle: distributional-shift surveys (e.g. arXiv:2606.16933, "A Unified Causal-Origin Taxonomy of Distributional Shifts in RL"; arXiv:2402.03590) treat "policy-induced state distribution shift" as a first-class shift category — the policy that gathers data for/consumes a representation changes over training, so any component fit on an early-policy distribution risks decay as later policy states diverge from what it was fit on. This is the general form of the concern the doc raises, confirmed as a recognized RL failure class, not a niche worry.
- **The most concrete evidence found is A-RMA** (Kumar et al. follow-up, "Adapting Rapid Motor Adaptation for Bipedal Robots," ashish-kmr.github.io/a-rma). RMA's phase-1 frozen base-policy/encoder pair is fit once; phase 2's adaptation module then has to track it. A-RMA's own diagnosis (search-snippet-level verification, not full-text-read): the **frozen base policy can fail on out-of-distribution scenarios** once the deployed adaptation-module estimates diverge from the phase-1 training distribution, and the fix required is a **third phase that unfreezes the base policy** and fine-tunes it against the adaptation module's real (imperfect) outputs. This is the closest published confirmation of the doc's predicted failure mode: freezing locks a representation/policy to the distribution it was trained on, and later-policy (or later-adaptation-module) states that drift from that distribution degrade performance until something is unfrozen again.
- Also relevant: "Policy-Induced Self-Supervision Improves Representation Finetuning in Visual RL" (arXiv:2302.06009) and the mixed frozen-vs-finetuned result reported in general RL representation-learning surveys — frozen representations "work well on visually rich tasks... but completely fail on simpler tasks like DeepMind Control" in at least one cited comparison (search-snippet level; source paper not independently confirmed by title in this pass — treat as directionally suggestive, not a hard citation, until re-verified).

**Verdict on Q2: the predicted failure mode is plausible and has partial published support (A-RMA's forced unfreeze-and-finetune phase is the strongest single data point), but no paper was found that isolates and quantifies "frozen-φ staleness" as its sole subject.** This supports treating it as a real risk to gate on, not a hypothetical: the health gate should watch for degradation in downstream policy performance (or a discriminability/coverage metric on φ_x's activations under the *current* policy's state distribution) as training progresses past the point where the policy has moved meaningfully from φ_x's pretraining data distribution — mirroring what forced A-RMA's third phase.

## Q3 — Precedent for offline-pretraining a Koopman/latent-dynamics AE on RL rollout logs, then using it downstream

**Precedent exists and is reasonably well established, though mostly in planning/world-model contexts rather than "frozen input to a from-scratch policy-gradient RL run."**

- **Lusch, Kutz & Brunton (2018), "Deep learning for universal linear embeddings of nonlinear dynamics"** — the canonical DeepKoopman reference. Trains an autoencoder to learn Koopman eigenfunctions from pre-collected trajectory data of a dynamical system, enabling long-horizon linear prediction in latent space. This is squarely "fit φ on logged trajectories offline," the closest single precedent for your φ_x pretraining step itself (as an autoencoder/Koopman-fitting procedure, not paired with RL).
- **"Learning from Reward-Free Offline Data: A Case for Planning with Latent Dynamics Models"** (arXiv:2502.14819 / latent-planning.github.io) — trains a latent dynamics model on reward-free offline logs, then plans with it downstream; reports it handles suboptimal offline data and adapts across environment configurations. This is the closest match for "offline-pretrained dynamics-aware latent used downstream for control," though the downstream consumer is a **planner**, not an RL policy trained from scratch on the frozen features.
- **Koopman operator + data-quality literature (2026 arXiv cluster: "Diagnostic Certificates of Data Quality and Regression Identifiability for Koopman Identification," arXiv:2605.09545; "Excitation of control-affine systems and Koopman error bounds," arXiv:2511.03734; "Control-Channel Informativity for Koopman EDMDc under Behavior-Policy Data," arXiv:2605.17966)** — this is the field's live concern with exactly the pitfall the brief asks about: Koopman/EDMD identification quality is characterized via **excitation/coverage of the regression data and behavior-policy informativity of the control channel**. The explicit finding across this cluster: EDMD-style Koopman fits made from data generated by one behavior policy (e.g., your "scripted-excitation" rollouts) can be poorly identified in directions of state/action space that behavior policy did not excite — i.e., **action-distribution mismatch between the offline-pretrain rollouts and what the RL policy later needs is a first-order, actively-studied failure mode**, not a fringe worry. Verification depth: title/abstract-snippet level for all three; recommend a follow-up full-text pass if the doc needs a specific error-bound citation.
- **Established offline-Koopman-for-control tooling** (pykoopman, EDMD/DMD libraries referenced across the above cluster) routinely warns about exactly this: fit quality depends on the input signal's spectral richness (excitation), and models built from narrow/scripted excitation generalize poorly outside that excitation's coverage — directly analogous to why the doc proposes combining logged **and** scripted-excitation rollouts rather than logs alone.

**Verdict on Q3: precedented as a general technique (offline-fit latent-dynamics/Koopman models, later consumed downstream), with an active and directly-relevant sub-literature on the exact pitfall named in the brief — data coverage / excitation and behavior-policy-induced action-distribution mismatch degrading the identified operator/embedding outside the fitting data's support.** The Koopman-specific work is arguably the single best-grounded piece of evidence in this whole investigation (multiple 2025-2026 papers converge on the same diagnosis), and directly motivates a coverage/excitation check as part of φ_x's offline-fit health gate — before it is ever frozen and handed to the RL policy.

## Bottom line — is the frozen-φ_x arm precedented / semi-novel / novel, and what should its health gate watch?

**Semi-novel as a combination, well-precedented in its parts.** No single paper does "pretrain a dynamics-aware lifting on offline+scripted-excitation logs → freeze → train an RL policy from scratch on the frozen lift," but each piece has real precedent: DeepKoopman-style offline fitting (Lusch et al. 2018) for the pretrain step, ATC (Stooke et al. 2021) and RMA (Kumar et al. 2021) for the freeze-and-build-on-top discipline, and A-RMA plus the general distributional-shift literature for the predicted staleness failure mode. Frame the arm in the doc honestly as adjacent-to-precedented rather than either "standard practice" or "unprecedented."

**Two things the health gate must watch, both evidence-backed:**
1. **Excitation/coverage of φ_x's fitting data vs. what the RL policy actually visits** — the Koopman data-quality cluster (Q3) shows this is where offline-fit dynamics embeddings silently degrade; a coverage or reconstruction-error check on the *policy's current* state/action distribution against φ_x's training distribution is the direct mitigation this literature recommends.
2. **Policy-induced distributional drift over training time** — the A-RMA precedent (Q2) shows a frozen upstream component can go stale as the policy that consumes it evolves; track downstream policy performance (or φ_x activation/likelihood under the live rollout distribution) over training and treat a divergence trend as the trigger to consider unfreezing/fine-tuning φ_x, mirroring A-RMA's forced third phase.

## Implications for ALBC

- The frozen-φ_x arm should be documented as a **hybrid of established techniques applied in an under-published combination**, not "standard practice" and not "unprecedented" — closest structural analog is RMA's frozen extrinsics encoder (Kumar et al. 2021) combined with DeepKoopman-style offline fitting (Lusch et al. 2018).
- Before freezing φ_x, apply the Koopman-literature coverage/excitation check (Q3) to the offline+scripted-excitation dataset it is fit on — this is the field's most concrete, actively-researched failure mode for exactly this step, and directly relevant given ALBC's own DR/thruster-fault distribution is wide.
- Build the health gate around the A-RMA-style staleness signal (Q2): track downstream ConstraintTRPO+IPO policy performance (or a coverage metric comparing live rollout states to φ_x's fitting distribution) over training, and treat sustained divergence as the trigger to reconsider the freeze — mirroring the third unfreeze-and-finetune phase A-RMA needed once its own adaptation-module-driven distribution moved away from the frozen base policy's training distribution.
- ATC's (Stooke et al. 2021) frozen-encoder success is a useful existence proof that "frozen pretrained representation + separately-trained RL policy" *can* work well, but it is visual/pixel evidence — do not cite it as direct support for the state-based dynamics-lift case without noting that distinction explicitly in the write-up.
