# R3 — Reconstruction vs. Context Preservation Under Domain Randomization

## Q1: Direct evidence — reconstruction-weight ablation + context/parameter probing under multi-dynamics data

**Most direct hit found: "What Can Latent World Models Know? Physical Parameter Identifiability in Multimodal Predictive Representations"** (arXiv 2607.27017, read via HTML full text). This paper runs almost exactly the requested experiment: it trains a pixel-**reconstruction** objective and a **latent-prediction** objective on the same encoder trunk, under environments with varying physical parameters (stiffness, drag, mass), then linear/ridge-regression **probes** the resulting latents for parameter decodability (R²), with an independent "recoverability certificate" computed directly from raw observations as an upper bound on what's physically present.

Findings (quoted/paraphrased from the fetched text):
- For a **fast, high-signal parameter** (stiffness): probe R² = 0.50–0.62 from the learned latents vs. a certificate of 0.87 — partial recovery.
- For a **slow, low-variance parameter** (drag): probe R² = 0.12–0.13 for **both** objectives, vs. a certificate of 0.89 — i.e., the information is present in the raw trajectory (per the certificate) but neither reconstruction nor prediction extracts it.
- Explicit statement: "a pixel-reconstruction variant is at least as blind [to drag] as the latent-prediction objective... the failure sits with the deterministic prediction objectives we test, not with the environment or the probes."
- They also tested reweighting the loss toward the rare high-signal frames (32%→97% of loss share) and found decodability stayed pinned near 0.13 — i.e., **turning up the relevant-signal weight did not rescue parameter recoverability**, suggesting the bottleneck is architectural/objective-shape, not just loss-weight magnitude.

Interpretation for the exact ALBC question (varying reconstruction *weight* and measuring context info): this paper doesn't sweep a reconstruction-loss coefficient per se, but it does the adjacent and arguably more informative experiment — comparing reconstruction against a pure-prediction objective head-to-head with a ground-truth recoverability ceiling — and finds **reconstruction is not reliably better than prediction at preserving low-variance/slow parameter-correlated signal**; both under-perform what's physically available. This is evidence AGAINST the premise that adding a reconstruction term automatically "counteracts" predictor invariance pressure for context that is subtle relative to the dominant observation variance.

**Theoretical grounding — Voelcker et al. 2024 ("When does Self-Prediction help? Understanding Auxiliary Tasks in RL", arXiv 2406.17718, read via HTML full text):**
- Proposition 2: stationary points of the reconstruction loss span the **top-k singular vectors of the transition/observation covariance structure** (`span(Φ*) = span({u_1,...,u_k})`). This is a variance-driven, reward/task-agnostic selection rule.
- Proposition 4: when the raw observation is passed through some (possibly information-losing) observation function 𝒪, the reconstruction-optimal subspace is governed by the singular vectors of `𝒪⁻¹ P^π 𝒪`, which "lacks clear relationship to the original reward structure."
- Insight 3: latent self-prediction is preferable as an auxiliary task "especially with distracting processes," while reconstruction dominates only as a **standalone** objective in low-distraction settings.

**What this predicts for theta-correlated features that are low-variance in observations:** directly negative for the ALBC setting. If a finite-capacity reconstruction bottleneck (a 9D `z`) is optimized to span the top-k singular directions of `o_t`'s covariance, and the DR-parameter-correlated signal is a **low-variance perturbation riding on top of much larger-variance nominal-dynamics/state variation** (which is very plausible for hydrodynamic/thruster-fault parameters vs. the raw pose/velocity/attitude channels that dominate variance in `o_t`), then reconstruction will preferentially encode the high-variance directions and may not allocate latent capacity to the low-variance context-correlated directions **unless they happen to coincide with top singular vectors of the joint (state, θ)-induced observation covariance**. This is a quantitative, falsifiable prediction: with `encoder_latent_dim=9`, the theory says only the top ~9 variance directions of `o_t` survive optimally under pure reconstruction — theta must contribute non-trivially to one of those top directions to be preserved, and there's no a priori guarantee of that (in fact heavy DR randomizes many state-affecting quantities that likely dominate over subtler parameter perturbations like drag/thruster-gain coefficients — mirroring exactly the 2607.27017 "drag is a slow, low-variance signal that gets dropped" result).

## Q2: Dreamer lineage — do reported probes/motivations show reconstruction-trained latents fail to encode env parameters?

**PrivilegedDreamer (arXiv 2502.11377, "PrivilegedDreamer: Explicit Imagination of Privileged Information for Rapid Adaptation of Learned Policies", ICRA 2025)** — fetched as page images via the pdftoppm route (text-layer PDF fetch failed/returned binary garbage; PNG-page read succeeded, pages 1–3 and 6 read in full).

**Motivation section (Introduction, page 1) states explicitly:**
> "Our initial hypothesis was that the Dreamer framework could capture parameterized dynamics accurately by conditioning the model on latent variables, leading to better performance at the end of learning. However, Dreamer is designed to predict action-conditioned dynamics in the observation space and **does not consider the effect of hidden parameters**."

Page 3 continues:
> "Even though the RSSM has memory to determine the underlying dynamics, prior works... have shown that this hidden state information is **poorly captured implicitly and must be learned explicitly**."

And on the risk of relying on reconstruction/prediction losses alone to instill parameter awareness (page 3, discussing why the hidden-variable loss term alone underperforms):
> "it is important to highlight that relying solely on this hidden parameter loss term is not sufficient. Theoretically, it seems like the loss encourages the recurrent state variables h_t and z_t to encapsulate all relevant information... However, in practice, this privileged information remains somewhat indirect to those networks. Consequently, this indirect access hinders their ability to capture subtle changes and results in suboptimal performance."

**This is a direct, explicit statement from a Dreamer-lineage paper that the base world model's implicit (reconstruction-driven) latent does NOT reliably capture hidden dynamics parameters, motivating an explicit parameter-estimation+prediction module.**

**Quantitative ablation (page 6, Fig. 4 and Fig. 5, Table II referenced):** they compare three variants —
- `Dreamer + Decoder` (i.e., **vanilla DreamerV2's RSSM latent `h,z` with a separately-trained decoder probing for the hidden parameter ω** — this is functionally a linear/MLP probe on a reconstruction-trained latent),
- `Dreamer + Decoder + ConditionedNet`,
- full `PrivilegedDreamer` (adds the explicit LSTM estimator + prediction head).

Results: "Dreamer + Decoder" has the **highest** (worst) hidden-parameter reconstruction error of the three across Pendulum/Throwing/Pointmass (Fig. 4), and in Fig. 5's online estimation, "Dreamer + Decoder" **converges to wrong values in Pendulum and Pointmass** and takes >500 steps to converge in Pointmass, vs. the full model converging within ~5% of ground truth almost immediately. This is a controlled, in-paper probing ablation showing that **reconstruction-trained RSSM latents under randomized/parameterized dynamics carry weak, slow-to-extract, and sometimes wrong parameter information** relative to a model with an explicit parameter-prediction auxiliary task.

Caveat: this is not a sweep of reconstruction *weight*, and DreamerV2's reconstruction target is the full observation (state `x_t`), so it is closer to the ALBC L_KI setup than a pixel-based Dreamer variant — good analogy strength. No report found of a reconstruction-weight sweep specifically in the Dreamer lineage (DayDreamer, DreamerV3-in-DR papers) with parameter-probing; searches did not surface such an ablation. The related "Dreaming of Many Worlds" / cRSSM paper (arXiv 2403.10967) is adjacent but pursues the **opposite** design goal — it explicitly conditions the decoder on a *separate* context input and reports (via qualitative counterfactual visualization only, no formal MI/probe numbers) that the resulting RSSM latent becomes **disentangled from** (i.e., does NOT redundantly encode) context, contrasting with a concat-context baseline where context info leaks redundantly into the latent. That paper is evidence that when context is supplied as a side-channel, reconstruction pressure alone is not what carries context info — architecture/conditioning determines it, not reconstruction per se.

## Q3: Bottom line for ALBC — net pressure on theta-correlated features under L_KI = 0.75·rec + 0.1·pred-ls + 0.5·pred-ss

**Unknowable without a probe, but the available evidence leans negative-to-fragile, not confidently positive.** Three converging lines:

1. **Theory (Voelcker 2406.17718):** reconstruction optimizes for top singular vectors of the observation covariance — a variance-driven, not task/context-driven, selection. If theta's imprint on `o_t` is a small-variance perturbation relative to state/attitude/velocity variance under DR, it is not guaranteed a top-9 slot in a 9D bottleneck.
2. **Direct probing ablation (2607.27017):** reconstruction and prediction objectives showed **statistically indistinguishable, both-poor** recovery (R²≈0.12–0.13) of a slow/low-variance physical parameter (drag), against a much higher information-theoretic ceiling (0.89) — i.e., reconstruction did not rescue what prediction alone missed, for exactly the low-variance-parameter case that matches the ALBC concern.
3. **Dreamer-lineage motivation + ablation (PrivilegedDreamer 2502.11377):** explicit, paper-stated finding that reconstruction-trained latents "poorly capture implicitly" hidden dynamics parameters, with a controlled probe-decoder ablation showing worse/slower/wrong parameter recovery from a pure reconstruction+RSSM latent than from a model with an explicit parameter-estimation objective.

None of these three sources studied ALBC's exact recipe (weighted sum of full-obs reconstruction + short/long-horizon latent self-prediction, 9D latent, 6-DOF UUV thruster-fault DR), so a numeric prior (e.g., "expect R²≈0.1–0.3 for fault-related theta components without probing") would be an extrapolation, not a citation — flagged explicitly as such rather than asserted. The literature does **not** support the ALBC prior that a 0.75-weighted reconstruction term is intrinsically sufficient to counteract the predictor's shared-dynamics invariance pressure for low-variance context features; it supports the opposite default (reconstruction is necessary-but-unproven, not sufficient) and converges with the earlier R2/Q1 finding already in the design doc (Voelcker) that self-prediction-heavy mixes are the ones known to be fragile under distraction/invariance pressure specifically. **Actionable implication: the only way to know ALBC's actual disposition is the probe** — train a linear/ridge regression from `z` (or `z` at fixed points in training) to the DR fault/parameter ground truth, exactly as 2607.27017 and PrivilegedDreamer's Fig. 4/5 do, rather than trusting the reconstruction-weight ratio as a proxy.

## Implications for ALBC

- Do not treat "reconstruction weight is dominant (0.75) in L_KI" as evidence that theta-correlated features survive in `z`. The cited theory and two independent probing studies point the other way for low-variance parameter signal specifically — the exact regime ALBC's thruster-fault/hydrodynamic-coefficient DR likely falls into relative to dominant pose/velocity variance in `o_t`.
- The PrivilegedDreamer ablation design (linear/MLP decoder probe from `h,z` → ω, compared against an explicit-estimation variant) is a direct, cheap-to-replicate template for an ALBC-side probe: attach a small regression head on frozen `z` predicting the known DR fault parameters, train it post-hoc, and read its R²/error — this is the "get the actual answer" path the literature converges on, rather than reasoning from loss-weight ratios.
- If ALBC's probe result later turns out low, the literature also converges on a candidate fix already implicit in `2` and `3`: an explicit low-weight *parameter-prediction* auxiliary head (not just reconstruction) measurably rescues recoverability in the one paper that ships a comparable ablation (PrivilegedDreamer, though CLAUDE.md's `constrained-albc` "No Encoder Auxiliary Losses" rule currently forbids exactly this class of addition — flagging the conflict for awareness, not recommending violating the project rule).
